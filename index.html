Noah Cylich

I make large models run in less memory than they should need.

CS at UCLA, finishing this December. Most of what I do comes back to one observation, that only a fraction of a transformer matters for any given token, and you can spend that fact on speed or on memory. I have spent two years spending it.

Where I have worked

Ordered by when I joined. Open any of them for what I actually did there.

  1. Nov 2025
    Cactus ComputeML engineer and researcher

    An open source inference engine for phones, wearables, and anything else without a datacenter behind it. Third largest contributor across 53 merged pull requests. Almost everything I know about inference I learned here, mostly by being wrong first.

    • Gemma 4, fully multimodal on launch day. Wrote the model end to end and shipped every modality the day it went public, which nobody outside Google managed. Most of that week went on the precision fixes that let the audio encoder survive Apple's Neural Engine.
    • ARM decode kernels. Made small matmuls 3x faster, which lifted Gemma 4 E2B decode by 50%. Separately wrote a hybrid INT8/FP16 attention kernel that doubled attention throughput during decode without moving accuracy.
    • Quantization. Co-authored the INT4 weight kernels behind the engine's low-bit decode path.
    • Long context on a phone. Built KeyDiff KV-cache compaction and native chunked prefill, which made 8k-plus tokens usable where it previously was not.
    • More models. Whisper v3 and Parakeet-TDT speech recognition, with Parakeet accelerated on the Neural Engine, plus the Nomic mixture-of-experts text embedder.
    • Cross-device benchmarking across Mac, Samsung and Pixel, which is where the LM-head layout change and the Android core scheduling work came from.
    • The Go cloud backend that every cloud hand-off request from the engine terminates at.
    • LoRA merging in Tunix. Worked with Google's Tunix team to add merging and Hugging Face export for Gemma and Qwen models directly into their engine.
    • Papers. CLAWS as first author, plus HiDRA, TACE, and a controlled study of attention-only transformers.
  2. Jun 2025
    GoogleSoftware engineer intern, twice

    Two summers, two teams that had almost nothing to do with each other.

    • App Ads, 2026. An agentic pipeline for extracting and generating game imagery using Gemini and SAM 2, plus a caching layer over the video annotation system.
    • Database, 2025. An automated presubmit testing framework in C++ for Napa, the exabyte-scale database sitting under things like AdSense, so no schema change ships unvalidated. Also added repeatable read support so DeepMind's queries against it stayed consistent.
  3. Sep 2024
    PLUS Lab, UCLALLM researcher

    Professor Peng's lab. Her NLP course gave me the historical grounding I had been working without. I knew transformers and had no idea how the field arrived at them, and those older techniques still turn up in the analysis I do now.

    • Temporal Mixture-of-Experts. An MoE trained so consecutive tokens reuse all but one expert, which lets serving memory scale with active parameters instead of total. The interesting part came after training. The router had quietly reorganised, and its experts had stopped keying on words.
    • IE Benchmark. A benchmark for where LLMs fall down on information extraction.
